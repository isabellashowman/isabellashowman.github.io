Research

I use satellite remote sensing to observe permafrost change and sea ice movement. Upon starting graduate school, I hope to expand on what I have already done to include modeling in an effort to better understand ocean-atmosphere-ice interactions. Below is an overview of my main research projects.

Gas emission crater detection in Siberian permafrost

Unique crater features began appearing in the Siberian permafrost in 2014, but the mechanisms driving the gas emission crater eruption remain contested. Working with Dr. Alex Turner, I am finding ways to identify these crater features using Landsat 4-9 and Sentinel-2 imagery, a necessary precursor to ask future questions about why they form.

Sea ice retreat near river mouths

Our understanding of how landfast sea ice retreats on small-scales is limited by cloud-filled imagery. I used Sentinel-1 SAR to observe sea ice melt timing as a proxy for freshet event timing in recent history, working to improve our understanding of how river flux output influences sea ice break-up.

Dissolved oxygen vulnerability thresholds for key Puget Sound species

I completed a comprehensive literature review and analysis on marine species dissolved oxygen vulnerability thresholds, synthesizing values to contextualize human nutrient input threats in Puget Sound.
